Work that the lab's tools can lead to. Some of it is for looking at, some explains how it was made. Add things you find.

## Music and sound {#music}

- **Watch:** [Herbie Hancock jams with his Fairlight CMI](https://youtu.be/n6QsusDS_8A). A jazz pianist playing one of the first sampling synthesizers, an early digital instrument.
- **Watch:** [Modular Synth (not a spaceship, this is an instrument)](https://youtu.be/ZVPL78D7zF4) by Rob Scallon. A guitarist's tour of a modular synthesizer, which is the idea VCV Rack is built on.
- **Watch:** [1969: Introducing the Moog Synthesiser](https://youtu.be/RpuV-kpXZRI) from BBC Archive. A television report from when the synthesizer was new.
- **Watch:** [Generative ambient music in SuperCollider](https://youtu.be/YWtQUnMZN70) by Tomer Baruch. Music made by code that never plays the same way twice, in one of the lab's tools.
- **Watch:** [Moritz Simon Geist and his sonic robots](https://www.electronicbeats.net/the-feed/sonic-robots-video-feature/) from Electronic Beats. A tour of the workshop where robots play electronic music, and how they shape the way he works.

## Influential electronic artists {#artists}

Starting points for people whose work shaped electronic music. Each link goes to a reference page, so look up their records and live sets from there.

- **Read:** [Kraftwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraftwerk). A German group from Düsseldorf that built electronic pop from synthesizers, drum machines and machines of their own design, starting in the 1970s.
- **Read:** [Aphex Twin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphex_Twin). The name Richard D. James records under. He builds his own instruments and software, and his music runs from ambient to some of the most intricate electronic rhythms.
- **Read:** [Caterina Barbieri: Minimalism, Modular and Live](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/caterina-barbieri-minimalism-modular-and-live/) from Ableton. An Italian composer who makes long, hypnotic pieces from modular synthesizers and sequencers, with an interview about how she plays them live.
- **Read:** [Suzanne Ciani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Ciani). An American composer and a pioneer of the Buchla synthesizer, in concert halls and in sound design for film and advertising.
- **Read:** [Hiroshi Yoshimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshi_Yoshimura). A Japanese composer of quiet, spacious "environmental music" made to sit in a room rather than be listened to head-on. His album *Green* (1986) is a good place to start.

## Physical computing and kinetic art {#physical}

- **Explore:** [Wooden Mirror](https://vimeo.com/7820888) by Daniel Rozin. A camera and hundreds of small motors tilt wooden tiles to draw your reflection.
- **Watch:** [Sculpting waves in wood and time](https://www.ted.com/talks/reuben_margolin_sculpting_waves_in_wood_and_time) by Reuben Margolin, a TED talk. Sculptures where one motor moves hundreds of points to make a wave, with an explanation of how they work.
- **Watch:** [My creations, a new form of life](https://www.ted.com/talks/theo_jansen_my_creations_a_new_form_of_life) by Theo Jansen, a TED talk. The wind-driven walking sculptures called Strandbeests, designed with code and built from plastic tubing.

## Code as art {#code}

- **Explore:** [p5.js examples](https://p5js.org/examples/). Short sketches in JavaScript that run in the browser, each with its code beside it.
- **Explore:** [Hydra](https://hydra.ojack.xyz/). Live-coded visuals in the browser. Type a few lines and the picture changes as you type.
- **Watch:** [The Coding Train](https://thecodingtrain.com/) by Daniel Shiffman. Friendly video tutorials for making art with p5.js and Processing.
